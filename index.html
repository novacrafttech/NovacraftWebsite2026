DPDP Act compliance

DPDP compliance services for banks and financial institutions

India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules, 2025 change how banks, co-operative banks and fintechs collect, use, retain and protect personal data. Novacraft Technologies helps you move from gap assessment to examiner-ready evidence, alongside your RBI, NABARD / C-SITE and CERT-In obligations.

What is the DPDP Act, 2023, and what are the DPDP Rules, 2025?

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is India’s law on processing digital personal data. It sets duties for organisations (Data Fiduciaries) and gives individuals (Data Principals) rights over their data. The DPDP Rules, 2025, which make the Act operational, were notified on 13 November 2025.

When do DPDP compliance obligations start?

Implementation is phased. Some provisions took effect on notification in November 2025, the consent-manager provisions follow in November 2026, and the core compliance framework, including notice, consent, security safeguards, breach reporting and cross-border transfer rules, is scheduled for 13 May 2027. Timelines can change, so we confirm the current position at the start of every engagement.

Does the DPDP Act apply to banks and co-operative banks?

The Act applies to organisations that process digital personal data in India, and banks and co-operative banks are not exempt as a class. It sits alongside sector rules such as RBI cyber security guidance, NABARD / C-SITE requirements and CERT-In directions, so the practical task is mapping which obligations apply to your data and systems and closing the gaps.

What does DPDP compliance involve in practice?

Typically a data inventory across core banking, cloud and branch systems; privacy notices and consent flows; retention schedules; a workflow for data-principal rights and grievances; security safeguards and breach-response readiness; vendor and processor contract reviews; staff training; and an evidence pack you can show examiners.

What are the penalties for non-compliance with the DPDP Act?

Penalties are set by the Data Protection Board of India according to the nature and gravity of the breach and can reach up to ₹250 crore for the most serious failures, such as not maintaining reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach.

How does Novacraft Technologies help with DPDP compliance?

We run DPDP implementation together with RBI, NABARD / C-SITE and CERT-In compliance, from gap assessment and consent and notice design to retention, rights and grievance workflows, an audit calendar and an examiner-ready evidence pack.
